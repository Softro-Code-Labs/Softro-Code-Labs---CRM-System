# Softro CRM user guide

Softro CRM is where Softro Code Labs keeps track of every client, every enquiry and every project we're trying to win. This guide explains how to use it day to day.

> **The golden rule:** if it's about a client and it isn't in the CRM, it didn't happen. Log enquiries, update stages and record outcomes as you go, so the whole team sees the same picture.

## 1. Signing in

1. Open the CRM address your admin gave you (for example `https://crm.softrocodelabs.com`).
2. Enter your work email and password, then click **Sign in**.
3. You stay signed in for 7 days on that browser. To sign out, click the **arrow icon** at the top right.

**Your first sign-in:** your admin gives you a **temporary password** (it looks like `Softro-k7Mq-9xTe-Rw4p`). After signing in with it you'll be asked to **choose your own password** before you can use the CRM.

**Password rules:** at least 10 characters, with letters and numbers, and not containing your email name. A short sentence works well, e.g. *Galle sunset 2026*.

**Two-step sign-in:** if you've turned it on (see [section 19](#19-your-profile-and-password)), after your password the CRM asks for the **6-digit code** from the authenticator app on your phone. Lost your phone? Click **Use a recovery code** and type one of the codes you saved.

**Too many wrong passwords:** after 5 wrong tries your account is locked for 15 minutes. Wait, or ask an admin to unlock it. Lots of wrong attempts from one office network also pause sign-in from there for a few minutes.

**Forgot your password?** Ask an admin to **reset** it. You'll get a new temporary password and must choose a new one when you sign in.

**Check what you typed:** click the **eye** at the end of any password box to show the password, and again to hide it.

**On a shared computer?** Always sign out when you're done.

## 3. Your role and what you can do

Each person has one role. Ask an admin if you need more access.

Leads, deals and quotations (and the invoices and receipts that go with them) follow one rule everywhere: lists, pages, PDFs, the dashboard and reports.

| Role | Sees |

| **Sales, Support, Viewer** | Only records **they own or created**. Never another salesperson's. |
| **Manager** | Their own records **and their team's** (the people an admin has put under them). Not other teams. |
| **Admin** | **Everything**, including company-wide reports. |

- **Your team:** an admin sets each person's **Manager (team)** under **Settings → Team members**.
- **New website enquiries** are the exception: they wait on the shared **New web leads** board, which everyone sees, until someone takes one (see [section 5](#5-leads-new-enquiries)).

## 4. Your daily routine

A good routine takes about 10 minutes:

1. **Open the Dashboard.** Check **My tasks for today** and **Leads waiting for a reply**. If the leads card is red, some leads are past our 2-working-day promise.
2. **Reply to waiting leads** (WhatsApp, call or email), then **log it** in the lead's Activity box. Logging a call, WhatsApp, email or meeting marks the lead as replied for you.
3. **Work through your tasks** and tick each one off. Schedule the next follow-up before you move on.
4. **Open Deals → My deals.** Move any deal whose situation has changed to the right stage. Look for cards with a **red close date** (overdue) or many days in the same stage.
5. **Open Support → Mine.** Reply to anything marked *Reply overdue* or with an amber reply time first, and log each reply on the ticket.
6. **Log anything new** from yesterday: a WhatsApp enquiry, a referral, a new contact at a client.

## 5. Leads: new enquiries

A **lead** is someone who has asked about our services but hasn't agreed to a proposal yet. Every WhatsApp message, website form, referral or LinkedIn enquiry about a project should become a lead.

### Our reply promise: 2 working days

Our website promises a reply **within 2 working days**. The CRM tracks this for you:

- Every new lead gets a **reply-by deadline**: 2 working days later, Monday to Friday, Sri Lanka time. Enquiries made at the weekend count from Monday morning.
- The lead shows **Reply in 1 day** (grey), **Reply in 5 hours** (amber, less than a day left) or **Reply overdue** (red).
- The deadline stops counting as soon as someone clicks **Mark as replied**, **logs a call, WhatsApp, email or meeting** on the lead, or moves it to *Contacted* or *Qualified*.

> Public holidays and poya days aren't counted yet. Around holidays, reply sooner rather than later.

### Lead statuses

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **New** | Nobody has replied yet |
| **Contacted** | We've replied and are talking to them |
| **Qualified** | It's a real project with a real need, and worth a proposal |
| **Unqualified** | Not a fit (budget, timing, not something we do, no response). Always give a reason |
| **Converted** | Turned into a deal. The lead is now read-only and links to its deal |

### New web leads board

Enquiries from the website form don't belong to anyone at first. They wait on **Leads → New web leads** (the button shows how many are waiting), which **everyone** can see.

1. Open the board, read the enquiry and click **Take this lead**.
2. The lead is now **yours**. From then on only you, your manager and admins can see it.
3. If two people click at the same moment, the first one gets it and the other sees who took it.

You must take a board lead before you can edit it, mark it replied or change its status. **Convert to deal** on a board lead takes it at the same time. Admins can work board leads without taking them, for example to assign one to someone.

### Tabs on the Leads page

- **Open:** New, Contacted and Qualified leads. The default view.
- **Needs reply:** open leads nobody has replied to yet, most urgent first. The red number shows how many.
- **New / Contacted / Qualified / Converted / Unqualified / All:** one status at a time.

Use the **source** and **service** dropdowns and the search box to narrow the list.

### Add a lead

1. **Leads → New lead.**
2. Fill in:
   - **Title:** a short description of the project, e.g. *Booking system for a salon in Galle*.
   - **What do they want to build?** Pick the closest service.
   - **Source:** WhatsApp, Website form, Referral, LinkedIn and so on.
   - **Estimated value and currency:** a rough budget if you know it.
   - **Contact name, WhatsApp, phone, email:** at least **one** way to reach them is required.
   - **Preferred language:** English or Sinhala. Reply in their language.
   - **Business name / Existing company:** if we already have them as a company, link it.
   - **Their idea / requirements:** paste their message or notes from the call.
3. If you've **already replied** (e.g. you answered on WhatsApp straight away), tick **I've already replied to them**.
4. Click **Create lead**.

### Work a lead

On a lead's page:

- **WhatsApp:** opens WhatsApp with a greeting already typed, in Sinhala if that's their language. Local numbers like `077 123 4567` work automatically.
- **Mark as replied:** do this after your first reply. Stops the deadline.
- **Qualified:** they have a real need and want to hear how we'd build it.
- **Not a fit:** asks for a short reason, then marks it Unqualified. **Reopen lead** brings it back.
- **Edit:** change any detail, the owner or the status. Sales can only keep a lead themselves; a manager can give it to someone in their team; an admin to anyone.

### Convert a lead to a deal

When a qualified lead wants a proposal or quote, convert it:

1. Click **Convert to deal**.
2. **Client company:** choose one:
   - **Existing company:** pick it from the list. The CRM suggests one if the business name matches.
   - **Create new company:** uses the business name they gave.
   - **No company:** for individuals.
3. Leave **Create a contact for …** ticked to copy their name, email, phone, WhatsApp and language into Contacts.
4. Check the **deal name, value, currency, service and starting stage** (usually *Discovery call*).
5. Click **Convert to deal**. You're taken to the new deal.

## 6. Deals: projects we're trying to win

A **deal** is a specific project we're working to win, with a value and a stage.

### Our pipeline stages

The stages follow how Softro works with clients:

| Stage | What it means | Move here when… |
|---|---|---|
| **Discovery call** (20%) | We're learning about their idea, users and goals | A meeting or call about requirements is booked or done |
| **Proposal & quote** (50%) | We've shown how we'd build it and what it costs | The proposal or quote has been sent |
| **Design approval** (80%) | They're reviewing or approving screens before we code | They've agreed in principle and are approving designs |
| **Won** | Signed, paid deposit, or written go-ahead | Use **Mark won** |
| **Lost** | They said no or went elsewhere | Use **Mark lost** and give the reason |

The % is the typical chance of winning at that stage. It's used for the forecast.

### The deals board

**Deals** opens the board: one column per stage, one card per deal.

- **Move a deal:** drag its card to another column. On a phone, use the **stage dropdown** on the card.
- Each card shows the **value**, **service**, **owner**, **days in this stage** and **expected close date** (red if it has passed).
- Column headers show the **number of deals and total value** for that stage.
- At the top:
  - **Open pipeline:** total value of all open deals.
  - **Weighted forecast:** each deal's value × its stage %, a realistic estimate of what we'll win.
  - **Won this month:** deals marked won since the 1st, Sri Lanka time.
- **All deals / My deals:** switch between everyone's deals and only yours.
- **List** view shows deals as a table, including **Won** and **Lost** ones.

**Currencies:** deals can be in LKR, USD, EUR, GBP, AUD or AED. Totals are shown **per currency** (e.g. *LKR 850,000 + $1,200*). Different currencies are never added together.

### Add a deal directly

Usually deals come from converting a lead. To add one directly (e.g. a repeat client): **Deals → New deal**, or **New deal** on a company or contact page so the client is filled in for you.

### On a deal's page

- **Stage bar:** click a stage to move the deal.
- **Mark won:** closes the deal as won. If the company was a *Prospect*, it automatically becomes a **Customer**.
- **Mark lost:** asks for a reason (e.g. *went with a cheaper freelancer*). Reasons help us learn, so be honest and specific.
- **Reopen deal:** brings a won or lost deal back to the board.
- **Edit:** value, service, company, main contact, expected close date, owner, scope and notes.
- **Client card:** the company and main contact, with email, call and **WhatsApp** buttons.

## 9. Activities, tasks and notes

Every lead, deal, company and contact page has an **Activity** box. It's the history of everything we've said and done with that client, plus what's coming up.

### Add to the timeline

The box has three tabs:

| Tab | Use it for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Note** | Background or decisions the team should know | *Prefers to talk after 6 pm. Budget approved by their director.* |
| **Log a call, chat or meeting** | Something that **already happened**: a call, WhatsApp chat, email or meeting | *WhatsApp: sent the proposal PDF, they'll reply Friday* |
| **Schedule a task** | Something **to do later**, with a due date and time, an owner and a priority | *Call: follow up on the quote, tomorrow 10:00* |

- Scheduled tasks default to the **next working day at 10:00**. Change the date and time as needed (Sri Lanka time).
- You can assign a task to a colleague. It then shows in *their* task list.
- **High** priority tasks get an amber label.

### How it fits together

- Something logged on a **deal** also appears on that deal's **company** and **contact**, so a company page shows the whole relationship.
- A **deal** also shows everything from the **lead** it came from, so nothing is lost when you convert.
- On a **lead**, logging a call, WhatsApp, email or meeting counts as **our reply** and stops the 2-working-day clock.

### Upcoming and history

- **Upcoming:** open tasks, soonest first. Overdue ones are in **red**. Click the **circle** to tick one off.
- **History:** everything done and every note, newest first.
- To delete a note or activity, hover over it and click the **bin** icon. You can delete your own; managers and admins can delete anyone's.

### The Activities page

**Activities** in the sidebar is your to-do list across all clients:

| Tab | Shows |
|---|---|
| **Overdue** | Open tasks past their due time (red count) |
| **Today** | Due later today, plus open tasks with no date |
| **Upcoming** | Due tomorrow or later |
| **Done** | Completed in the last 30 days |

Switch **Mine / Everyone** to see the whole team's tasks, and filter by type (Call, WhatsApp, Email, Meeting, Task). Each item links back to its lead, deal, company or contact.

## 10. Quotes

A **quote** (quotation) is the price we send a client for a project: line items, totals and our terms, as a branded PDF.

### Create a quote

1. Open the **deal** and click **New quote** in the *Quotes* box. The title, client and contact are filled in for you. (You can also use **Quotes → New quote**.)
2. Check the **client company** and **Attention of** (contact). Every quote needs a company or a contact.
3. Add **line items**:
   - **Add from catalogue…** adds a standard service with its price (see *Services & prices* in [section 20](#20-settings-admins-and-managers)). You can still change the price for this client.
   - **Add line** adds a blank line for anything custom.
   - For each line set the **name**, optional **details** (printed under the name), **qty**, **per** (project, hour, month…), **price** and optional **line discount %**. Use the arrows to reorder.
4. Set a **discount %** for the whole quote, and **tax** if we charge it (e.g. *VAT 18%*). The totals update as you type.
5. Check the **quote date**, **valid until**, **introduction** and **terms**.
6. **Internal notes** are only for the team and are **never printed**.
7. Click **Create quote**. It gets the next number (e.g. **Q-2026-0007**) and starts as a **Draft**.

- **Terms:** leave a **blank line** between sections (Payment, Validity, How we work). Each section is kept together on the PDF: if it doesn't fit at the bottom of a page, the whole section moves to the next page instead of being split.
- Quotes **don't print bank details**. Those go on invoices only, so don't write "bank details are on this quotation" in the terms.

### Send it

- **View PDF** opens the quote in a new tab. **Download PDF** saves it (e.g. *Q-2026-0007 Lanka Spices.pdf*) so you can attach it to an email or send it on WhatsApp.
- After sending it, click **Mark as sent**. A note is added to the deal's timeline.

### Track the answer

| Status | Meaning | Buttons |
|---|---|---|
| **Draft** | Being prepared, not sent yet | Edit, Mark as sent, Duplicate, Delete |
| **Sent** | With the client | **Accepted**, **Declined**, Back to draft, Edit |
| **Accepted** | Client said yes | Undo, New version |
| **Declined** | Client said no | Undo, New version |

- **Accepted** also sets the deal's **value** to the quote total and adds a note to the deal. Then move the deal on (e.g. *Design approval*) or **Mark won**.
- A sent quote past its *valid until* date shows **Past valid date**. Follow up or send a new version.
- **Accepted and declined quotes are locked.** To change prices, click **New version**. It copies the quote as a new draft with a new number and today's date.
- Every quote PDF ends with two signature blocks: one for us, and **Accepted by the client** (name, signature, company seal and date). Ask the client to sign and send it back before you start work. Keep the signed copy with the project files and attach it to the deal as a note.
- Once a quote is accepted, invoice it from the quote's page (see [section 11](#11-invoices-and-receipts)).

### Seal a quote, invoice or receipt (admins and managers)

Admins and managers can stamp a document with the **company seal** and their **signature**. It prints in the *For Softro Code Labs* box with their name, job title and the date it was sealed.

1. **Once:** add your signature under **My profile → Signature** (see [section 19](#19-your-profile-and-password)), and check your **job title** under *Your details*.
2. Open the quote or invoice and click **Apply seal** above the document. For a receipt, click **Seal** next to the payment on its invoice.
3. Download the PDF as usual: it now shows your signature, the seal and your name and title.

- The seal shows the **company registration number** from **Settings → Company details** (or *OFFICIAL* if it's empty).
- A **sealed document is locked**: it can't be edited, deleted, voided or cancelled. To change it, an admin or manager clicks **Remove seal**, makes the change and seals it again.
- The name, title and signature are saved with the document when it's sealed, so later changes to your profile don't change documents already sealed.
- Other roles see that a document is sealed and who sealed it, but can't seal or unseal. Every seal and removal is in the audit log.

### Tax and currency

- Quotes can be in **LKR, USD, EUR, GBP, AUD or AED**. Prices aren't converted, so check catalogue prices if you change the currency.
- Our default tax rate is set in **Settings → Company details**. If unsure whether to charge VAT on a quote (for example for overseas clients), ask your manager or accountant.

## 11. Invoices and receipts

An **invoice** asks the client to pay. A **receipt** confirms we received the money. Together with the quote and the Project Delivery Pack, these are the only papers most Sri Lankan clients need from us.

### Invoice an accepted quote (the usual way)

1. Open the **accepted quote**. An **Invoices** box shows how much of it is still to invoice.
2. Choose what to invoice:
   - **Advance (%)**: for example 50% before we start. The invoice has one line, *Advance payment (50%)*, plus VAT at the quote's rate.
   - **Full amount**: copies every line of the quote.
   - **Balance**: after an advance, invoices whatever is left. Use **Another part payment** for milestone payments (e.g. 30% / 40% / 30%).
3. Click **Create invoice**. It gets the next number (e.g. **INV-2026-0012**), today's date, and a due date from **Settings → Company details** (14 days unless changed).

The advance and balance invoices always add up to the quote total, VAT included. The CRM won't let you invoice more than the quote.

### Other invoices

- **Maintenance, hosting or support:** open the **contract** and click **Invoice this period**. It invoices one period's fee (monthly, yearly…).
- **Anything else** (extra work, a domain renewal): **Invoices → New invoice**, or **New invoice** on a won deal. It works like the quote form.

### Send it

- **Download PDF** and send it by email or WhatsApp. The PDF has our bank details (only invoices show them, not quotes) and the amount in words (e.g. *Rupees Two Hundred Thirty-Six Thousand Only*).
- If we're **VAT registered** (our VAT number is filled in under Settings), the PDF is titled **Tax Invoice** and shows our TIN and the client's. Put the client's VAT/TIN number in their **company** record (*Tax / BR number*) before invoicing a VAT-registered client.
- You can **edit** an invoice until any money is received, and as long as it isn't sealed. If the client already has it, send the new PDF.
- To add the company seal and your signature, see [Seal a quote, invoice or receipt](#seal-a-quote-invoice-or-receipt-admins-and-managers).

### When the client pays

1. Open the invoice. Under **Record a payment**, enter the **amount**, **date received**, **how** (bank transfer, cash, cheque, card, online) and the **reference** (bank reference or cheque number).
2. Click **Record payment**. The CRM makes a numbered **receipt** (e.g. **RCT-2026-0009**). Click **Receipt PDF** and send it to the client.
3. If they paid part of it, the invoice shows **Part paid** and the balance. When the balance reaches zero it shows **Paid**.

You can't record more than the balance. Receipts for cheques say *subject to realisation*.

### Chasing payment

- Each unpaid invoice has a **Follow up payment** task for the deal owner, due the day after the due date. It closes itself when the invoice is paid.
- **Invoices** shows *Owed to us*, *Overdue* and *Received this month*. The **Unpaid** and **Overdue** tabs list the oldest first.
- The dashboard's **Money owed to us** box lists overdue invoices.

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Unpaid** | Sent, nothing received, not due yet |
| **Due soon** | Due within 3 days |
| **Overdue** | Past the due date and not fully paid |
| **Part paid** | Some money received |
| **Paid** | Fully paid |
| **Void** | Cancelled; kept on record |

### Mistakes

- **Wrong payment?** A manager or admin clicks **Cancel** next to it (remove the receipt's seal first if it has one). If it was the **latest** receipt, its number is freed and the next payment gets it again (cancel RCT-2026-0001 and the next is RCT-2026-0001). If newer receipts exist, the number stays unused, so there's no duplicate. Either way, if the client already has that receipt, tell them to ignore it.
- **Wrong invoice?** If nothing has been paid and it isn't sealed, a manager or admin can **Void** it, then create the right one. Invoices are never deleted, so the numbers stay in order for the accountant.
- **Voided by mistake?** A manager or admin opens it and clicks **Restore invoice**. It's unpaid again with the same number. The CRM won't restore it if the quote has been invoiced again since, because that would bill the client twice: void the replacement first.
- **Something the screens can't undo** (a wrong number, test invoices on the live CRM): admins use the ready-made scripts in [DATABASE-FIXES.md](DATABASE-FIXES.md).

> **Ask the accountant** to check one Tax Invoice before you send your first, in case they want anything else on it.

## 12. Contracts: maintenance, hosting and support

A **contract** is ongoing paid work for a client after a project: website maintenance, hosting, a support retainer, a software subscription. Contracts make sure renewals are never missed and show how much recurring income we have.

### Add a contract

Go to **Contracts → New contract**, or click **New contract** on a company, or on a deal once it's **Won**.

| Field | What to enter |
|---|---|
| **Title** | Short and clear, e.g. *Website maintenance & hosting* |
| **Company** | Required. Contracts always belong to a company. |
| **Type** | Maintenance, Support, Hosting, Retainer, Subscription or Other |
| **Start and end date** | The period the client has paid or agreed for. Leave the end date empty for open-ended contracts. |
| **Amount and billing** | e.g. LKR 25,000 **Monthly**. The Contracts page converts everything to a monthly figure for the total. |
| **Remind me (days before end)** | Days before the end date to start the renewal (default 30) |
| **First response within (hours)** | The response time we promised. Tickets under this contract use it. Leave empty for our standard times. |
| **Support hours included / month** | If the contract has a monthly limit |
| **Scope** | What's included and what isn't. This settles arguments later. |

Each contract gets a number like **C-2026-0001**.

### Renewal reminders

When a contract has an end date, the CRM creates a **high-priority task** for the contract owner: *Renew contract C-…*, due on the reminder date. It shows on their dashboard and in Activities. If you change the dates, the task moves with them.

The contract shows **Ends in N days** in amber once the reminder date has passed, and **Past end date** in red if nobody renewed it.

### Renew, pause or cancel

On the contract's page:

- **Renew for another period** creates the next contract (same terms, new dates, new number), marks the old one **Ended**, ticks off the renewal task and moves any open tickets to the new contract. You land on the new contract's edit page: update the price or scope if they changed.
- **Pause** for a client who has stopped temporarily. **Reactivate** brings it back.
- **Cancel contract** when the client has left. It asks you to confirm.

### The Contracts page

Tabs: **Active**, **Renewing soon**, **Ended** and **All**. The cards at the top show active contracts, **monthly recurring value** (per currency) and how many are due for renewal now.

## 13. Support tickets

A **ticket** is one client problem or request: *"Checkout page shows 500 error"*, *"Please add new product photos"*. Log every request that comes in by WhatsApp, email, phone or in person, so nothing is forgotten and response times are tracked.

### Create a ticket

Go to **Support → New ticket**, or click **New ticket** on a company or contract.

- Choose the **company** or **contact**, and the **contract** if the work is covered by one. Tickets without a contract may be billable.
- Set the **priority** and where it **came in** (WhatsApp, email…).
- The ticket is assigned to you unless you pick someone else. It gets a number like **T-2026-0001**.

### First-reply times (SLA)

Every ticket has a **first reply due** time. If the contract has a promised response time, that is used. Otherwise:

| Priority | First reply within |
|---|---|
| Urgent | 4 hours |
| High | 24 hours |
| Medium | 48 hours |
| Low | 72 hours |

The badge on the ticket shows the time left, e.g. **Reply in 3 hours** (amber when under 4 hours), **Reply overdue** in red, and afterwards **Replied on time** or **Replied late**.

### Work a ticket

- **Log your replies.** Write what you told the client and keep **"This is a reply sent to the client"** ticked. The first reply stops the SLA clock and moves the ticket to *In progress*.
- **Internal notes:** untick the box. Use these for causes, fixes and anything the team should know.
- Use the **WhatsApp** button to message the contact directly.
- Change the **status** with the buttons at the top: *Open → In progress → Waiting on client → Resolved → Closed*. Use *Waiting on client* when you need something from them.
- Change who it's **assigned** to with the dropdown on the right.

### The Support page

Tabs: **Open**, **Mine**, **Reply overdue**, **Waiting on client**, **Resolved** and **All**, with a priority filter and search. Open tickets are sorted by priority, then by the reply deadline.

## 14. Project access and delivery

Every project gets its **own Google account** (a Gmail), and every service for that project is signed up with it: Vercel, GitHub, the database, hosting and so on. Use **Continue with Google** (or **Continue with GitHub**) where a service offers it. Otherwise sign up with the Gmail and its own password, and save that password in Google Password Manager. Signing in to Chrome with that Gmail then brings back all its passwords and bookmarks, so **you don't need to keep a separate browser profile** for each project.

The CRM keeps the **map**: which Gmail, which services, who owns them and when they renew. **Passwords, keys and 2-step codes are never typed into the CRM.** They stay in Google Password Manager, and the CRM refuses text that looks like one. The only time you type passwords is to make the **login sheet** for the client, and they aren't saved even then.

### Set it up

Open a **won deal** and click **Project access → Set up** (or **Project access** in the sidebar for all projects).

| Field | What to enter |
|---|---|
| **Project Gmail** | e.g. `galle.hardware.softro@gmail.com` |
| **2-step verification** | Use an **authenticator app**. Save its setup key and backup codes in the password manager, so it doesn't depend on one person's phone. |
| **Recovery email / phone** | **Company** ones (admin@softrocodelabs.com and the office number), never personal |
| **Password and backup codes are kept in** | *Where* they are, e.g. "Gmail password in Google Password Manager (Softro admin) · backup codes in company Drive → Security" |
| **Settings (.env) are kept in** | e.g. "Vercel project settings". For Vercel projects, `vercel env pull` rebuilds the file. Otherwise keep a copy in the project's Google Drive, which has version history. This text appears in the Project Delivery Pack. |

Then **Add a service** for each account: domain, hosting, Vercel, GitHub, database, email, payments… Give its address, login email, how you sign in (*Continue with Google*, *Continue with GitHub*, *Email + password* or *Other*), **who owns it** (Softro, the client or shared) and, for anything paid, **when it renews**. A renewal date creates a reminder task 30 days before.

### Keep it alive: the 6-monthly check

Google can delete personal accounts that haven't been used for 2 years, and clients often come back after a year. So every 6 months the deal owner gets a task: **sign in to the project Gmail, check the 2-step codes work, then press "I signed in and checked it."** The **Project access → Needs a check** tab lists any that are overdue.

### When the project is finished

Work through the **close-out checklist**: logins saved, backup codes saved, settings saved, client handed over, **browser profile removed**, local `.env` files deleted. Then click **Archive project access**. Nothing is deleted. When the client asks for maintenance, press **Reopen for maintenance**, sign in to the project Gmail in Chrome, and everything is back.

### What the client gets: two documents

**1. Project Delivery Pack** (no passwords). **Create Project Delivery Pack** makes a numbered document (e.g. **DP-2026-0001**) with the project Gmail, every service, who owns each one, renewal dates, support details and a sign-off section. It's saved, so you can download it again exactly as it was sent. Email it freely.

**2. Login sheet** (the passwords). At the bottom of the Project access page:

1. Type the **Gmail password** and, if you're handing them over, the unused **backup codes**.
2. Type the password for each service that uses *Email + password* or *Other*. Services that use *Continue with Google* or *Continue with GitHub* don't need one; the sheet says how to sign in. Leave a box empty to give that password another way.
3. Choose a **PDF password** (8+ characters), e.g. something the client will recognise.
4. Click **Download login sheet**. The PDF is locked with that password (AES-256), and the form clears itself.

**Nothing you type is saved**: not in the database, not in the audit log (it only records that a sheet was made). If you need it again, make a new one.

Send the file by email or WhatsApp, and tell the client the **PDF password separately**, by phone or in a different chat, never in the same message. Ask them to change each password after they take over.

## 15. Reports

**Reports** shows how sales, leads, the team and support are doing. Use it in the weekly team meeting and at month end.

What you see depends on your role: **Sales** see only their own numbers ("Just you"), **managers** their team's ("Your team"), and only **admins** see the whole company. Contract revenue in *Support and contracts* follows the same rule (contracts owned by you or your team).

### Choose what to look at

- **Period:** last 12 months (the default), this or last month, quarter or year, or **Custom dates…** for any range.
- **Everyone you can see** or one person. Picking a person filters sales, leads and the tickets assigned to them. Managers can pick anyone in their team; admins anyone.

Money charts use **LKR**. Deals in other currencies (e.g. USD) are listed separately and never added to LKR.

### What's on the page

| Section | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| **Sales** | Won revenue, **win rate** (won ÷ won and lost), average deal size and average days to win. A chart of won revenue per month, plus revenue by **service**, the **top clients**, and **why we lost** (from the lost reasons people entered). |
| **Pipeline forecast** | Open deals by **expected close month** for the next six months. The light column is the full value; the dark part is the **weighted forecast** (value × the stage's win probability). Also how many deals have a **close date that has passed** or **no close date**: fix those so the forecast is right. |
| **Leads** | New leads, how many became deals, how many were **replied to within 2 working days**, the typical reply time, and a table of **where leads come from** and which sources turn into won work. |
| **Team** | For each person: leads, replies on time, deals won and lost, win rate, won value, open pipeline, tasks done and tickets resolved. |
| **Support and contracts** | Tickets opened and resolved, **first reply on time**, typical first-reply time, tickets by priority, **monthly recurring value** of active contracts and renewals due in the next 90 days. |

Hover over (or tap) a column to see its exact value.

### Export to Excel or Google Sheets (admins and managers)

Click **Export CSV** and choose **Deals**, **Leads**, **Support tickets** or **Team performance**. The file uses the same period and person you have selected, and only contains records you're allowed to see (a manager's export is their team's). Open it in Excel or import it into Google Sheets. Every export is recorded in the audit log.

### Keep the numbers honest

Reports are only as good as what's entered. Always mark deals **Won** or **Lost** (with a reason), keep **close dates** and **values** current, set the **service** on each deal, and log replies to leads and tickets.

## 19. Your profile and password

Click **your name** at the top right to open **My profile**:

- Update your **name, job title and phone / WhatsApp**.
- See your **role** and what it allows.
- **Profile photo:** click **Add a photo** (or drag one onto the circle). It's cropped to a square and shown next to your name across the CRM. Use **Change photo** or **Remove** any time. Hidden details in the file, like where the photo was taken, are removed automatically.
- **Change your password:** enter your current password, then the new one twice. This signs you out on **every other device** (handy if you used a shared computer).
- **Signature (admins and managers):** used when you seal a quote, invoice or receipt. Click **Draw it** to sign with your mouse, finger or pen, or **Upload a photo** of your signature on plain white paper in dark ink: the paper background is removed automatically. Your **name and job title** from *Your details* print under it. Documents you've already sealed keep the signature they were sealed with.

### Appearance: dark or light

The CRM opens in **dark mode**. To change it, click the **moon icon** at the top right (or go to **My profile → Appearance**) and choose:

- **Dark**, the default, easy on the eyes;
- **Light**, best in bright rooms;
- **Match my device**, which follows your computer or phone and switches with it.

Your choice is saved on that browser, so you can use dark on your laptop and light on your phone. Quote previews always stay white, like the printed PDF.

### Two-step sign-in (recommended for everyone)

With two-step sign-in, a stolen or guessed password isn't enough to get into the CRM: you also need your phone.

1. Install **Google Authenticator** or **Microsoft Authenticator** on your phone (free).
2. In the CRM, go to **My profile → Two-step sign-in → Set up two-step sign-in**.
3. In the app, tap **+** and **scan the QR code**. (Or type the key shown next to it.)
4. Type the **6-digit code** the app shows for *Softro CRM* and click **Turn on**.
5. **Save your 10 recovery codes.** Click **Download** or **Copy** and keep them in the company password manager, or print them. Each one lets you sign in once if you lose your phone. They're only shown once.

Turning it on signs you out of your other devices. From now on, sign in with your password and then the code from the app (it changes every 30 seconds).

- **New phone?** Before you wipe the old one, use **Turn off** and then set it up again on the new phone. Or sign in with a recovery code and do it then.
- **Running low on recovery codes?** Click **New recovery codes**. The old ones stop working.
- **Lost your phone and your recovery codes?** Ask an admin to **reset two-step sign-in** for you, then set it up again.
- If the company requires it for your role (admins and managers), you'll be asked to set it up before you can use the CRM, and you can't turn it off.

## 20. Settings (admins and managers)

**Settings** appears in the sidebar for admins and managers.

### Team members (admins)

- **Add team member:** enter their name, work email, job title and role. The CRM creates a **temporary password** and shows it **once**. Click **Copy sign-in message** and send it to them privately (e.g. on WhatsApp). They'll set their own password on first sign-in.
- **Change role:** open the person, pick a new role and **Save changes**. They're signed out so the new permissions apply straight away.
- **Manager (team):** for Sales, Support and Viewer, choose their manager. That manager then sees their leads, deals, quotations and numbers, and can assign leads to them. Admins and managers aren't in a team. If a manager changes to another role, their team members have no manager until you choose a new one.
- **Reset password:** gives them a new temporary password and signs them out everywhere. Use it when someone forgets their password.
- **Unlock sign-in:** shown when someone is locked out after too many wrong passwords.
- **Remove photo:** takes down someone's profile photo if it isn't suitable. They can add a new one.
- **Reset two-step sign-in:** for someone who lost their phone *and* their recovery codes. It signs them out; they set it up again next time. Check it's really them first (call them).
- **Deactivate:** for people who leave. They're signed out and can't sign in, but everything they created stays. Reassign their open deals and tasks first. **Reactivate** brings them back.
- Status labels on the list: **Active**, **Temporary password** (hasn't signed in and changed it yet), **Locked** and **Deactivated**.
- Safety rules: you can't deactivate yourself or remove your own admin rights, and there must always be at least one active admin.

### Sales pipeline (admins and managers)

- **Rename** a stage or change its **win %**, then click **Save** on that row.
- Use the **arrows** to change the order of open stages.
- **Add stage** puts a new stage just before *Won*.
- **Remove** a stage with the bin icon. Only stages with **no deals** can be removed, so move their deals first.
- *Won* and *Lost* are built in. You can rename them, but they're always last, at 100% and 0%.

### Company details (admins)

The details printed on every quote, invoice and receipt: company name, address, phone, email, website, **BR number** and **TIN/VAT number** (leave empty if not registered). It also holds the **quote defaults**: tax label and default rate, how many days quotes are valid for, **default terms**, **bank details** (printed on invoices only, never on quotes), and a footer line. The **BR number** is also printed on the company seal. Under **Invoices**: how many days clients have to pay (sets each invoice's due date) and the **payment instructions** printed on invoices.

> The default terms are only a starting point. Replace the [x] and [n] placeholders with Softro's real payment and revision terms before sending your first quote.

### Services & prices (admins and managers)

Your **catalogue** of standard services, e.g. *Business website (5 pages)*, LKR 150,000 per project, or *Hosting & support*, LKR 5,000 per month. Each has a name, price, currency, unit ("per") and an optional description that's printed on quotes.

- **Edit** with the pencil icon.
- **Archive** a service you no longer sell. It disappears from *Add from catalogue…* but old quotes keep it. **Restore** brings it back.

### Integrations (admins)

**Settings → Integrations** shows what's connected, with a test button for each:

- **Email (Resend):** needs `RESEND_API_KEY` and `EMAIL_FROM` set in Vercel. Use **Send me a test email** to check.
- **Team chat:** create an *incoming webhook* for a channel in Slack, Google Chat, Discord or Teams, paste its address, choose what to post, then **Send a test message**. The address is never shown again in full. Paste a new one to change it, or **Disconnect**.
- **Website enquiry form:** shows who gets the email alert for new enquiries (they wait unassigned on the New web leads board) and when the last one arrived. If it says "isn't a CRM user", fix `WEBSITE_LEAD_OWNER_EMAIL`.
- **Morning summary:** needs `CRON_SECRET` in Vercel. Shows when it last ran. **Send today's summaries now** sends them by hand (only once a day).
- **Accounting export:** accepted quotes, line by line with VAT, from **Reports → Export CSV → Accepted quotes**.

### Security (admins)

**Settings → Security** shows who has two-step sign-in on and how many recovery codes they have left.

- **Require two-step sign-in for admins and managers:** switch this on once you've set it up for yourself. Admins and managers who haven't set it up are asked to do it the next time they open the CRM. Other roles can still choose to turn it on.
- The page also lists the CRM's built-in protections (account lock, limits on sign-in attempts and website enquiries).

### System health (admins)

**Settings → System health** answers "is everything working?":

- **Database** status and how fast it responds. **Version** shows which release is live.
- **Plan usage:** how close we are to our hosting limits, as bars that turn amber at 80% and red at 95%:
  - **Database storage**, e.g. *9.7 MB of 1.0 GB*. It doesn't reset. If it fills up, saving new records fails until we free space or upgrade.
  - **Database compute** and **data transfer** this month, with a forecast for the month (needs the Neon API key, see the README). They reset each month. If compute runs out on the free plan, the database pauses until next month.
  - **What takes the space:** the largest parts of the database (profile photos, the audit log, activities…).
  - A link to Vercel's own usage page, because Vercel doesn't share usage with the app.

  At 80% or more, every admin's morning email includes a **Plan limits** warning.

Admins get **one alert per error per hour**, in team chat if connected, otherwise by email. When it's fixed, click **Mark fixed**. If it happens again, it comes back.
- **Configuration:** which settings are filled in (values are never shown).
- **Uptime and backups:** the address for an uptime monitor and how backups work.

If someone reports "Something went wrong", ask for the **Ref** number on the error screen. It matches the *ref* shown here.

### Audit log (admins)

A read-only record of every sign-in and every create, update and delete: who, what and when (Sri Lanka time). Filter by type of record or by person. Website enquiries show as *Website / system*.

## 21. Good habits and data rules

- **One lead per enquiry, one company per business.** Search before adding so we don't get duplicates.
- **Write titles people can scan:** *Mobile app for a tuition class in Kandy* beats *New enquiry*.
- **Keep stages current.** An out-of-date board gives a wrong forecast.
- **Log every client conversation** (one line is enough), and **always schedule the next step** before you leave a lead or deal.
- **Always give reasons** for *Not a fit* and *Lost*. They show where we can improve.
- **Set yourself as owner** of the leads and deals you're handling, so *My deals* is accurate.
- **Client data is confidential.** Don't export, screenshot or share it outside Softro. Don't store passwords, bank or card details in notes.
- **Use real data only.** For testing, use the test site, not the live CRM.
